Welcome

The REWORK project investigates whether remote working can significantly impact both the migration of individuals from metropolitan areas to peripheral regions and the labour force participation rate of women. The extent of this effect is hypothesised to depend on the level of state involvement in promoting remote work as a standard form of employment.

About the Project

The COVID-19 pandemic has profoundly impacted the adoption of digital and remote work practices, leading to significant changes in societal attitudes. This shift has granted employees greater control over their working hours and the freedom to choose their place of residence, while allowing employers to tap into previously inactive or marginalised segments of the labour market.

Given European markets' ageing population and labour shortages, remote work can activate the workforce, reduce social inequalities, and support local labour market development. The REWORK project evaluates the influence of remote work on the activation of women and the spatial mobility of young families.

Core Hypotheses

Hypothesis 1 — Regional Mobility
Does remote work drive relocation from urban to peripheral areas?
Does the implementation of remote work solutions in the post-pandemic era encourage individuals aged 25–49 to relocate from urban areas to peripheral regions?
Hypothesis 2 — Women's Participation
Does remote work motivate mothers to increase workforce participation?
Does remote work motivate women with children under the age of six to increase their professional activity — and equally so in peripheral regions as in metropolitan areas?

Why It Matters

Promoting women's professional engagement is a priority and key objective of the EU's economic policy. Increased female labour force participation fosters diversity and empowers women to support themselves, their families, and their communities. Furthermore, technological and social changes associated with remote work can serve as drivers of regional development and mitigate the negative consequences of urbanisation.

Research Tasks & Methodology

The project consists of five research tasks, combining primary and secondary empirical data using quantitative and qualitative methods. The main data source is a conjoint experiment survey conducted in six EU countries.

Task 1
Comparing remote working arrangements across EU countries
Systematic cross-country comparison of remote work uptake, regulation, and labour market outcomes across selected EU member states.
Task 2
Designing and conducting a conjoint survey
Development and implementation of an original conjoint experiment survey in France, Italy, Sweden, Romania, Poland and the Netherlands.
Task 3
Evaluating employee motivation to reside in peripheral areas
Investigating whether and how remote work motivates individuals aged 25–49 to relocate from metropolitan centres to peripheral regions.
Task 4
Investigating mothers' motivation to increase workforce participation
Examining how remote work affects professional activation of women with young children (under age 6) across different regional contexts.
Task 5
Determining whether remote work activates women equally across regions
Comparative assessment of remote work's potential to activate women in peripheral regions versus metropolitan areas.
